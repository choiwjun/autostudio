① Background

## A Brief History of Self-Publishing

The publishing industry is old, yet for most of its history, its model changed surprisingly little. The evident shift happened when digital technology appeared and started reshaping how books were created, distributed, and discovered.

Pre-2007

**Traditional gatekeepers**. Publishers controlled access to shelves.

2007

**Kindle + KDP launch**. For the first time, authors reach readers directly, no publisher required.

2011–13

**The Kindle Gold Rush**. Indie authors achieve real visibility and income with minimal marketing spend.

2014–19

**Paid promotion normalizes**. The platform matures; competition grows across every genre.

2020 — today

**3.5 million+ titles per year.** Indie publishing is a global phenomenon and (increasingly) a full business model.

Traditional

Breakout

Gold Rush

Platform era

Business era

But who exactly is driving this shift? And what does the typical indie author look like today?

② Who’s Publishing

## Indie Authors: An Overview

When we talk about self-publishing authors, we need to understand who these people are and why they choose the alternative to traditional publishing. Written Word Media, a book marketing agency, has been asking indie authors about their experiences with writing, publishing, and marketing since 2016. In 2025, they asked 1,346 authors how they identify themselves and which publishing format they prefer. Here are the numbers from their [2025 Indie Author Survey](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-indie-author-survey-results-insights-into-self-publishing-for-authors/):

| Finding | What It Tells Us |
| --- | --- |
| 77% | Identify as fully self-published; only 5% remain fully traditional |
| 40% | Publish primarily to make money; 20% to tell their story; 15% treat writing as a hobby |
| 98% | Publish eBooks; 97% also publish paperbacks, 51% audiobooks, 36% hardcovers |
| 83% | Continue publishing with Amazon as their primary platform |

These numbers help us understand the bigger picture and how the market evolves.

③ Market Size

## The Self-Publishing Market at a Glance

According to [Bowker](https://www.bowker.com/), _“The total number of books published in the U.S. in 2025 with ISBN numbers jumped 32.5% over 2024.”_ The number of self-published works also increased, with print and eBooks rising to 38.7%, from 2.5 million to more than 3.5 million in 2024.

Meanwhile, if we take a look at [global self-publishing market statistics](https://www.automateed.com/self-publishing-statistics), we can see the following:

01

**The global self-publishing market reached $1.85 billion in 2024 and is expected to reach $6.16 billion by 2033.**

02

**Traditionally published titles are decreasing compared to self-published ones: in the U.S. alone, 500,000 self-published works were released in 2023, compared to just 10,000 from traditional publishers.**

03

**Despite the volume, income remains highly concentrated: roughly 75% of self-published authors earn less than $1,000 per year, while only the top 0.5% earn six figures or more.**

04

**Technology adoption is streamlining the process: 87% of self-published authors tend to use print-on-demand, 65% use multi-platform distribution, and AI tool adoption is projected to jump from 42% today to 85% by 2030.**

As you can see, self-publishing is increasingly becoming a bigger share of the market, powered by direct sales, AI tools, and catalog monetization. It looks like the industry is shifting from simply publishing books to running book businesses.

Let’s see what’s driving the change.

④ Format Trends

## Digital Formats as the Drivers of Growth

We briefly discussed the rise of digital formats when we discussed [how EPUB changed digital marketing](https://isbndb.com/blog/epub-the-format-that-changed-digital-publishing/). According to a report from _Fortune Business Insights_ _( [Ebook Market Size, Share, and](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/ebook-market-111315)_ [_Industry Analysis By Platform, 2026-2034_ _)_](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/ebook-market-111315), in 2025, the U.S. eBook market accounted for roughly 33% of the $50.6 billion global eBook market and is expected to grow further.

While the [Association of American Publishers (AAP)](https://publishers.org/news/aap-december-2025-statshot-report-overall-publishing-industry-up-9-4-for-month-of-december-and-up-1-1-year-to-date/) reports show slight year-over-year fluctuations, eBook revenue has remained relatively stable and even grown modestly in recent years, increasing 1.5% in 2024 and showing overall gains over a five-year period.

Format in Focus · Audiobooks

A $2.22 Billion Market and Still Growing

At the same time, the digital audio format went up 2.1%, reaching $1 billion in revenue. The annual consumer survey from [Audio Publishers Association](https://www.audiopub.org/surveys) found that 51% of Americans aged 18 and older (around 134 million people) have listened to an audiobook. Among non-listeners, 38% mentioned they are interested in audiobooks. Another peculiar fact revealed through the survey is that AI-narrated audiobooks gain interest overall, though willingness to try them dropped from 77% in 2023 to 70% in 2025. Yet, the survey also reports audiobook revenue reaching $2.22 billion in 2024.

In other words, digital formats are growing and stabilizing; the demand for them is strong. Yet, the real disruption is now happening not exactly in format, but in how books reach readers.

⑤ Revenue Dynamics

## Direct Sales: The Income Divide

That shift in how books reach readers has a name: direct sales. Income numbers reported by indie authors in the [2025 survey by Written Word Media](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-indie-author-survey-results-insights-into-self-publishing-for-authors/) are as follows.

| Monthly Earnings | Share of Authors |
| --- | --- |
| $100 or less | 44% |
| Over $100 | 56% |
| Over $5,000 | 13% |
| Over $10,000 | 8% |

The same survey mentioned roughly 30% of indie authors already sell directly from their own sites. Among lower-earning authors, however, that number is smaller.

While more authors than ever are building their own platforms (websites, mailing lists, etc.), having a presence and selling from it are two very different things. The income gap tells the real story: roughly half of authors making $10,000 or more per month sell direct, while the majority of authors at lower income levels still rely entirely on retail platforms like Amazon.

> “Yet, despite the gap, the interest in self-publishing is growing: the survey shows that another 30% of authors who aren’t selling direct yet say they plan to start within the next 12 months. The appeal is clear: direct sales let authors keep up to 90–95% of each sale, own their customer data, and offer things retailers can’t (e.g., signed copies, bundles, or exclusive editions).”

⑥ Print Economics

## Self-Publishing and Print Books

Print remains nearly universal among indie authors, but the economics of selling it changed significantly in mid-2025, and not entirely in authors’ favor.

Platform Change · June 2025

KDP Royalty Cuts Changed the Math for Low-Price Titles

In June 2025, [KDP lowered royalty rates](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G8BKPU9AGVZSF9QF#royalty_calculation) for paperbacks and hardcovers priced below certain thresholds from 60% to 50%. A 300-page black-and-white paperback previously sold at $8.00 on Amazon.com earned $0.33 in royalties. But after the change, it might earn nothing because the minimum list price jumped from $7.67 to $9.20. To soften the blow, Amazon also reduced per-page printing costs in select markets, dropping standard color printing on Amazon.com from $0.0270 to $0.0255 per page. While it helps, it does not fully offset the royalty cut for most affected titles.

For authors selling at lower price points, the impact is real. With Amazon still being the top revenue source for 83% of indie authors, a change like this had a significant impact and served as a reminder that building on a single platform is a risk.

The June 2025 update accelerated what was already a growing interest in direct sales. For authors looking to offset that pressure without writing new titles, the answer might be in their existing catalog.

⑦ Strategy Shift

## Back Catalog as a Business Asset

One of the quieter but more significant shifts in indie publishing is how authors are thinking about their older titles. Rather than letting backlist books fade into obscurity, savvy authors are treating them as active revenue opportunities. They are refreshing covers, updating metadata, reworking book descriptions, and running targeted promotions to bring backlist titles back into discoverability.

They can go to Amazon KDP, Google Play, and Apple Books, or to platforms like Smashwords, Draft2Digital, IngramSpark, XinXii, StreetLib, PublishDrive, etc., to access tools that help authors optimize backlist titles for current search trends. For authors with a catalog of 10, 20, or 30 books, this isn’t a minor tactic. It is a meaningful revenue source that doesn’t require any new writing.

The Emerging Pattern

Active backlist management, together with direct sales and smarter distribution, points to a fundamental shift in what it means to be a self-published author today.

⑧ Conclusion

## The New Publishing Model: Running a Book Business

Self-publishing in 2025 is no longer just about getting a book out. It is about running a business. The most successful indie authors today operate across multiple revenue streams (e.g., retail platforms, direct storefronts, email lists) rather than relying on any single channel.

They invest in professional covers and editing, [use AI tools to their advantage](https://isbndb.com/blog/ai-in-publishing-industry/#ai-benefits-for-independent-authors), build reader relationships before launch day, and treat their backlist as an asset to be managed, not just a catalog to be archived. The line between “author” and “publisher” has effectively disappeared. What’s emerged in its place is something closer to an independent media business, built around a writer’s body of work.

## Final Thoughts

Self-publishing is no longer just an alternative. It is an increasingly chosen model for new titles and a growing force in global book revenue. It offers the best chances to those authors who are ready and willing to treat their writing like a business. For readers, it means more books, more voices, and more ways to buy. For the industry at large, it means the loss of relevance for the traditional gatekeeping model. The authors who will thrive in this environment are the ones who think like business owners and are ready to manage all aspects of publishing, including marketing.
