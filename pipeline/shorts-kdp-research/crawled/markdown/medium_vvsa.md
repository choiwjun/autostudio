What makes a YouTube Short go viral? How profitable are they? And do they contribute to channel growth? Today, I’ll unravel these mysteries based on a detailed study of 3.3 billion views across 5,400 shorts from 33 diverse YouTube channels. As your YouTube Wizard, I’ll provide an in-depth analysis of our findings. To keep updated on all things YouTube, don’t forget to subscribe to my newsletter.

## Key Insights from the Study

## Length Matters

Our study indicated that most creators make shorts between 20 and 40 seconds, but longer shorts performed better on average. Shorts with an average view duration of more than 50 seconds generated an average of 4.1 million views. Thus, the trick is not just to make longer shorts, but to hold the viewer’s attention as long as possible.

## The Role of the Algorithm

YouTube’s Shorts algorithm seems to favor longer videos that sustain viewer duration. However, we found outliers at all lengths, indicating you need not stick to a particular length to gain views.

## Viewed vs Swiped Away Ratio

YouTube recently added a new metric — the percentage of shorts viewed vs swiped away (VVSA). Our study showed that shorts with less than 60% VVSA performed poorly, while those between 70 and 90% VVSA did better. However, a high VVSA score doesn’t guarantee success, much like the click-through rate (CTR) for long-form videos. So, take this metric into consideration, but don’t obsess over it.

## Engagement Metrics

We did not observe a strong correlation between likes, shares, comments, and performance. These metrics might still be relevant for YouTube’s satisfaction scoring, and we will test this further.

## Are Shorts Worth It?

## Subscriber Growth

Initial findings indicated that long-form videos converted more subscribers per 10,000 views than shorts. However, channels with over 1 million subscribers had better conversion rates with shorts, possibly due to better content and call-to-actions in their shorts.

## Revenue

On the revenue front, the RPMs (revenue per 1000 views) of shorts aren’t particularly exciting, but they have more than doubled since the implementation of the new partner split. However, the length of the short did not seem to affect the RPM significantly.

## Conclusion

So, should you invest your time and effort in YouTube Shorts? Yes, because even though they’re not major money-makers (yet), shorts can significantly grow your subscriber base, especially if you already have a large following. Moreover, we expect the RPMs for shorts to rise over the next year.
